TIPS Anatomy & Indexation Mechanics

Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) are indexed to the non-seasonally adjusted US Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U). While traditional nominal Treasuries pay fixed nominal dollars that lose purchasing power when inflation accelerates, TIPS protect the investor's real purchasing power by escalating both principal and coupon payments with inflation.

Index Ratio & Adjusted Principal
The 3-Month Indexation Lag: Because the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) reports CPI data with a lag (e.g. May CPI is released mid-June), the US Treasury uses an official 3-month indexation lag with daily linear interpolation. For example, the reference CPI for July 1st uses the CPI index from April.
Semi-Annual Coupon & Redemption Principal (Deflation Floor)
The Embedded Deflation Floor: At maturity, the US Treasury guarantees redemption at $\max(\text{Par}, \text{Inflation-Adjusted Principal})$. Even if cumulative deflation occurs over the bond's lifespan, the investor is guaranteed to receive at least 100% of par face value ($1,000 per bond). This embedded put option has considerable value during deep disinflationary shocks.

Breakeven Inflation (BEI) & Term Structure

Breakeven Inflation (BEI) is the market-implied average annual inflation rate that equates the total return of a nominal Treasury with that of a TIPS of identical maturity. If actual CPI over the holding period averages above the breakeven rate, TIPS outperform nominal Treasuries; if actual inflation undershoots breakeven, nominal Treasuries win.

Fisher Equation & Breakeven Derivations
Decomposition of Breakeven Inflation: $$\text{BEI} = \mathbb{E}[\pi] + \text{Inflation Risk Premium} - \text{TIPS Liquidity Premium}$$
  • Expected Inflation $\mathbb{E}[\pi]$: The true consensus market forecast of future CPI.
  • Inflation Risk Premium: Extra return demanded by nominal bond investors for bearing inflation uncertainty ($+10\text{ to }+40\text{ bps}$).
  • TIPS Liquidity Premium: TIPS are less liquid than on-the-run nominal Treasuries, suppressing TIPS prices and raising their yields (reducing BEI by $-10\text{ to }-30\text{ bps}$).

Real Yield Curves & Negative Real Rates

The Real Yield Curve plots TIPS yields to maturity across tenors. It represents the real cost of capital for the US government and the real investment return available to investors after stripping out expected inflation.

Can Real Yields Be Negative? Yes! During the 2011–2013 European sovereign crisis and again throughout 2020–2021, the entire TIPS curve traded at negative real yields (with 10-year TIPS hitting a record low of $-1.20\%$ in August 2021).

Economic Implications:
  • Central Bank Financial Repression: Aggressive quantitative easing (QE) and zero-interest rate policies force yields below inflation.
  • Extreme Flight to Safety: Global investors accept guaranteed purchasing power destruction in exchange for sovereign liquidity and principal safety.
  • Investment Hurdle Rate: Ultra-low or negative real yields incentivize capital investment into riskier growth assets, equities, and real estate.

Inflation Trading Strategies & Swaps

On fixed income trading desks and macro hedge funds, portfolio managers trade inflation views using the Breakeven Trade and Zero-Coupon Inflation Swaps (ZCIS).

Long Breakeven (Long Inflation)
Long TIPS + Short Nominal Treasury
Duration-neutral (DV01 matched). Profits when breakeven inflation widens or actual CPI surges.
Short Breakeven (Short Inflation)
Short TIPS + Long Nominal Treasury
Duration-neutral (DV01 matched). Profits when inflation expectations collapse or disinflation takes hold.

Zero-Coupon Inflation Swaps (ZCIS)

Zero-Coupon Inflation Swap Net Payoff

In a Zero-Coupon Inflation Swap, Party A (inflation buyer) pays a fixed compounded swap rate $(1 + K)^T - 1$ at maturity $T$, and Party B (inflation seller) pays the cumulative percentage change in the CPI index $(\text{CPI}_T / \text{CPI}_0) - 1$. There are no interim cash flows, making it the purest synthetic derivative to hedge or speculate on cumulative inflation.

Inflation & Real Rates Interview Flashcards

10 essential interview questions on TIPS, breakeven inflation, deflation floors, real yield curves, and inflation trading.

1. What is a TIPS bond and how does its principal and coupon adjust for inflation? +
A TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Security) is a US sovereign bond whose principal is indexed to non-seasonally adjusted CPI-U. The principal is scaled by the Index Ratio ($\text{Reference CPI} / \text{Base CPI}$).

The stated coupon rate is a real coupon. The semi-annual dollar coupon payment is calculated as: $$\text{Coupon Payment} = \frac{\text{Real Coupon}}{2} \times \text{Inflation-Adjusted Principal}$$ As inflation compounds, both the principal and the coupon cash flows increase in dollar terms, preserving the investor's real purchasing power.
Desk Tip: Remember: the coupon rate stays constant; it is the principal base upon which the coupon is calculated that escalates.
2. What is the indexation lag on TIPS and why is it necessary? +
TIPS utilize a 3-month indexation lag with daily linear interpolation. For example, for any settlement date in July, the reference CPI is interpolated from the April and May CPI figures.

Why it's necessary: The BLS publishes monthly CPI figures mid-month for the preceding month (e.g. May CPI is released mid-June). Without a lag, market participants would not know the exact index ratio on transaction settlement dates, making secondary market trading and accrued interest calculation impossible.
Desk Tip: Because of this 3-month lag, TIPS do not hedge sudden, instantaneous monthly inflation spikes as effectively as short-term commodity futures or inflation swaps.
3. Explain the Deflation Floor on TIPS. Why does it possess option value? +
The Deflation Floor guarantees that at maturity, the redemption principal paid to the investor is: $$\text{Redemption Principal} = \max(\text{Par}, \text{Inflation-Adjusted Principal})$$ If cumulative inflation is negative over the life of the bond (deflation), the investor still receives $100\%$ of original face value.

Option Value: This guarantee represents an embedded European put option on the cumulative price index, struck at the base CPI. When inflation is low or the economy faces severe deflation risk, this put option increases in value, making newly issued TIPS trade at lower yields than unfloored inflation derivatives.
Desk Tip: The deflation floor is most valuable on newly issued TIPS (where the Index Ratio is near 1.0). On seasoned TIPS that have accumulated 20% inflation (Index Ratio = 1.20), the floor is deeply out-of-the-money.
4. What is Breakeven Inflation (BEI) and how is it calculated from market yields? +
Breakeven Inflation (BEI) is the rate of inflation that would make an investor indifferent between holding a nominal Treasury and a TIPS of the same maturity.

Linear approximation: $$\text{BEI} \approx Y_{\text{nominal}} - Y_{\text{real}}$$ Exact Fisher relationship: $$\text{BEI} = \frac{1 + Y_{\text{nominal}}}{1 + Y_{\text{real}}} - 1$$ If realized CPI exceeds the BEI, TIPS outperform nominal Treasuries.
Desk Tip: For quick mental math on the trading desk, traders almost always use the linear spread: $Y_{\text{nominal}} - Y_{\text{real}}$.
5. If a 10Y nominal Treasury yields 4.35% and 10Y TIPS yields 2.05%, what is the breakeven inflation rate? +
Linear BEI: $$\text{BEI} = 4.35\% - 2.05\% = 2.30\% \quad (230 \text{ bps})$$ Exact Geometric BEI: $$\text{BEI} = \frac{1.0435}{1.0205} - 1 = 1.022538 - 1 = 2.254\%$$ An investor expects average annual inflation to be ~2.25%–2.30% over the next decade. If the investor believes true inflation will average 3.00%, they should buy TIPS and sell nominals.
Desk Tip: State both the 2.30% linear rule-of-thumb and the 2.25% geometric exact calculation in interviews to demonstrate thorough quantitative mastery.
6. Can real yields be negative? What does a negative real yield mean economically? +
Yes, absolutely. US 10-year TIPS traded at deeply negative real yields between $-0.50\%$ and $-1.20\%$ in 2020–2021.

Economic Meaning:
  1. Purchasing Power Destruction: Investors accept a guaranteed slight loss in real purchasing power because safe nominal yields are held near zero by central banks while inflation expectations remain positive.
  2. Flight to Quality: Capital values sovereign default safety and liquidity over real yield.
  3. Financial Repression: The central bank actively suppresses nominal yields below inflation through quantitative easing (buying bonds) to stimulate borrowing and reduce real debt burdens.
Desk Tip: Negative real yields do NOT mean nominal yields are negative; nominal yields remain positive as long as expected inflation exceeds the absolute value of the negative real yield.
7. What components make up Breakeven Inflation beyond pure expected inflation? +
Breakeven Inflation is NOT purely expected inflation. It is decomposed into three distinct economic forces: $$\text{BEI} = \mathbb{E}[\text{CPI}] + \text{Inflation Risk Premium} - \text{TIPS Liquidity Premium}$$
  • Inflation Risk Premium (+10 to +40 bps): Investors dislike inflation volatility. Nominal holders demand an extra risk premium for holding fixed cash flows.
  • TIPS Liquidity Premium (-10 to -30 bps): TIPS have wider bid-ask spreads and lower daily trading volume than on-the-run nominal Treasuries. This liquidity haircut lowers TIPS prices, pushing TIPS yields up and thereby narrowing BEI.
Desk Tip: During market crises (e.g. March 2020), the TIPS liquidity premium explodes higher, artificially causing breakeven rates to crash even if inflation expectations haven't moved.
8. How does a desk structure a 'Long Breakeven' trade? +
A Long Breakeven trade expresses a pure macro view that inflation or inflation expectations will rise, while immunizing against broader interest rate moves.

Execution:
  • Buy TIPS (e.g. $50M 10Y TIPS).
  • Sell (Short) Nominal Treasuries with the same DV01 (dollar duration).
Outcome: If parallel real rates rise or fall by 50 bps, both legs offset each other ($\text{Net DV01} \approx 0$). However, if the breakeven spread widens by 25 bps, the position generates a clean profit equal to $\text{DV01} \times 25\text{ bps}$.
Desk Tip: Matching duration is insufficient—you must match DV01 (Dollar Duration), because TIPS trade at a different clean price and have differing coupon frequencies.
9. How does a Zero-Coupon Inflation Swap (ZCIS) work compared to a TIPS bond? +
A Zero-Coupon Inflation Swap (ZCIS) is an unfunded bilateral OTC derivative:
At maturity $T$, Party A pays a compounded fixed rate $(1 + K)^T - 1$, and Party B pays the actual cumulative CPI inflation rate $(\text{CPI}_T / \text{CPI}_0) - 1$.

Key differences vs TIPS:
  1. Unfunded: Requires zero upfront capital (unlike buying physical TIPS bonds).
  2. No Interim Cash Flows: A single net payment settles at maturity.
  3. No Deflation Floor: ZCIS contracts are unfloored; if deflation occurs, the inflation buyer pays negative floating cash flow to the seller.
  4. Clean Inflation Measure: Avoids TIPS liquidity premiums and sovereign funding frictions.
Desk Tip: The spread between the ZCIS swap rate and cash TIPS breakeven is called the Inflation Basis, driven by TIPS supply and balance sheet costs.
10. If inflation surges unexpectedly tomorrow, which outperforms: a newly issued TIPS or a seasoned TIPS with a high Index Ratio? +
A seasoned TIPS with a high Index Ratio will outperform in dollar terms, but in percentage terms their sensitivity depends on duration and coupon:

The Mechanics:
  • Because the seasoned bond's principal has already grown to say $1,250 per $1,000 par (Index Ratio 1.25), any new percentage CPI increase multiplies across a larger base of dollar capital!
  • Conversely, the newly issued TIPS has a much more valuable Deflation Floor (since its Index Ratio is 1.00), which acts as a downside cushion if inflation turns negative instead.
Desk Tip: For pure upside inflation sensitivity, traders prefer seasoned high-index-ratio bonds; for defensive downside protection, traders favor on-the-run newly issued TIPS.
