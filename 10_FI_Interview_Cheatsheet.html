Sales & Trading Desk Mental Math & Rules of Thumb

Essential mental math approximations and desk rules used daily by bond traders, structurers, and portfolio managers.

1. The Universal DV01 Mental Formula Sensitivity
For any standard bond trading near par, the dollar sensitivity per $100,000 face value for a 1 bp (0.01%) yield move is approximately:
DV01 per $100,000 Par ≈ $10 × Duration
Example: A 10-year Treasury with duration of 7.0 years has $\text{DV01} \approx \$10 \times 7 = \$70$ per $\$100\text{k}$ par ($=\$70,000$ per $\$100\text{M}$ position).
2. Price Change from Yield Move Risk
Quickly compute the expected dollar profit or loss on a position:
$\Delta \text{P\&L} \approx - \text{DV01}_{\text{position}} \times \Delta y_{\text{bps}}$
Example: You are long $\$50\text{M}$ 10Y Treasuries ($\text{DV01} = \$35,000$). If yields drop by $15\text{ bps}$, your profit is: $$\Delta \text{P\&L} = - \$35,000 \times (-15) = +\$525,000$$
3. Convexity Adjustment Mental Math Convexity
For large yield moves (e.g. 100 bps), duration alone underestimates price increases and overestimates price decreases. Add the convexity kicker:
$\Delta P_{\text{convexity}} \approx \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Convexity} \times (\Delta y)^2$
For a 10-year bond with convexity = 70 and a 100 bp (0.01) yield move: $$\Delta P_{\text{conv}} \approx 0.5 \times 70 \times (0.01)^2 = +0.0035 = +0.35\% \text{ of bond price}$$
4. The 6% CTD Crossover Rule Bond Futures
CME standardizes futures at a 6% coupon. When market yields deviate:
Yields > 6.00% → Highest Duration / Lowest Coupon is CTD
Yields < 6.00% → Lowest Duration / Highest Coupon is CTD
Because conversion factors under-discount when yields $>6\%$, long-duration bonds become mathematically cheapest to deliver.
5. Breakeven Inflation (BEI) Spread TIPS & Inflation
The breakeven rate is instantly estimated as the simple yield difference:
BEI (bps) ≈ Nominal Yield (bps) - Real TIPS Yield (bps)
Example: If 10Y nominal is 4.35% and 10Y TIPS is 2.05%, $\text{BEI} \approx 230\text{ bps}$ ($2.30\%$).
6. Par Swap Rate from Discount Factors Swaps
The par swap rate is simply the lost principal divided by the annuity sum:
$R_{\text{par}} = \frac{1 - DF_n}{\sum \alpha_i DF_i} = \frac{1 - DF_n}{A_n}$
If the 5-year discount factor is $DF_5 = 0.80$ and annuity $A_5 = 4.4$: $$R_{\text{par}} \approx \frac{1 - 0.80}{4.4} = \frac{0.20}{4.4} = 4.545\%$$
7. Rule of 72 for Zero-Coupon Bonds Compounding
To estimate how long it takes for a zero-coupon bond to double in price (or trade at 50% discount):
Years to Double ≈ 72 / YTM(%)
At 6% yield, a zero-coupon bond doubles in $72 / 6 = 12$ years (a 12Y zero trades near 50).
