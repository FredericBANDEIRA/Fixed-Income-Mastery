Interest Rate Swaps (IRS)

Swap anatomy, par rate derivation, negative swap spreads, OIS discounting, and swap DV01 sensitivity

Anatomy of an Interest Rate Swap

An Interest Rate Swap (IRS) is an over-the-counter (OTC) derivative contract in which two counterparties agree to exchange periodic interest rate cash flows calculated on a specified Notional Principal ($N$) over a predetermined tenor.

Position Pays Receives Market Jargon Directional Stance
Fixed Rate Payer Pre-agreed Fixed Coupon ($R_{\text{fixed}}$) Floating Benchmark (e.g. SOFR, Euribor) "Payer Swap" (Bought Swap) Bearish Rates: Profits if interest rates rise.
Floating Rate Payer Floating Benchmark (e.g. SOFR, Euribor) Pre-agreed Fixed Coupon ($R_{\text{fixed}}$) "Receiver Swap" (Sold Swap) Bullish Rates: Profits if interest rates fall.

The Golden Rule: No Principal Exchange

Unlike bonds or loans, the notional principal is never exchanged at inception or maturity. It serves purely as the multiplier for interest calculations. On each payment date, cash flows are netted:

Net Settlement Cash Flow Formula (Payer Perspective)

Why Institutions Trade Swaps

  • Corporate Borrowers: Issue floating-rate bank loans or commercial paper, then enter a Payer Swap to synthesize fixed-rate long-term debt.
  • Commercial Banks: Hold long-duration fixed mortgages; enter Payer Swaps to convert fixed assets to floating to match floating deposit liabilities.
  • Pension Funds & Life Insurers: Enter Receiver Swaps to add synthetic 30-year duration to match ultra-long liabilities without deploying massive cash capital.

The Par Swap Rate & Mark-to-Market Valuation

At inception, an interest rate swap is an exchange of two commitments of equal value. Therefore, the initial Net Present Value (NPV) of a swap is exactly ZERO:

Deriving the Par Swap Rate

A floating-rate note (FRN) paying benchmark coupons and redeeming principal at maturity $T$ is always worth par ($1.00$) on every reset date. Thus, the present value of all floating coupons equals:

PV(\text{Floating Leg}) = 1.00 - DF_n

Swap Spreads & The Negative Spread Anomaly

The Swap Spread is the difference between the benchmark par interest rate swap rate and the yield of the on-the-run government Treasury bond of the same maturity:

Swap Spread Definition

The Historical Evolution

  • Pre-2008 Paradigm: A swap rate was universally expected to be higher than the Treasury yield ($\text{Swap Spread} > 0$, typically $+30$ to $+80\text{ bps}$) because swaps were linked to interbank lending (Libor), which carried AA-rated bank credit risk vs. default-free sovereign Treasuries.
  • Post-2015 Anomaly: Negative Swap Spreads! Today, long-term US swap spreads (10Y and 30Y) consistently trade NEGATIVE (e.g. 30Y swap spread $\approx -30\text{ to } -60\text{ bps}$).
⚠️
Why Did 30-Year Swap Spreads Go Negative?
  1. Treasury Supply Overhang: Massive US federal budget deficits led to record Treasury issuance, pushing physical 30Y bond yields UP.
  2. Regulatory Balance Sheet Costs (Basel III / SLR): Primary dealers face steep capital charges for holding physical Treasury bonds on their balance sheets. Synthetic derivatives (swaps) do not consume cash balance sheet capacity!
  3. Pension & LDI Hedging Demand: Pension funds aggressively receive fixed on 30Y swaps to hedge duration, pulling the 30Y swap rate DOWN relative to Treasuries.

OIS Discounting & The Multi-Curve Framework

Prior to the 2008 global financial crisis, trading desks used a simple single-curve framework: Libor swap rates were used to both project future floating cash flows AND discount them back to present value.

When interbank credit spreads exploded during the Lehman collapse, the industry realized that collateralized bilateral derivatives (governed by Credit Support Annexes — CSAs) earn the Overnight Risk-Free Rate (interest paid on posted cash collateral). This birthed the Dual-Curve (OIS Discounting) Framework.

Tenor Basis Swaps

A Basis Swap exchanges two floating benchmark rates (e.g., 3-Month SOFR vs. 1-Month SOFR, or 3M Euribor vs. 6M Euribor). The spread quoted on one leg is the Basis Spread, reflecting liquidity preference and credit risk differences across tenors.

Swap Risk Management & DV01 Sensitivity

How sensitive is an interest rate swap to parallel and non-parallel curve moves? Because a swap has no principal exchange, its interest rate risk is derived entirely from the difference between the duration of its two legs:

Payer vs Receiver Risk Profiles

  • Payer Swap (Short Fixed): Equivalent to Short Fixed Bond + Long Floating Note. Has negative duration ($\approx -D_{\text{fixed}}$). Gains when yields rise; loses when yields fall.

Sales & Trading, Structuring & CFA L1/L2 Interview Flashcards

These questions are core staples in fixed income derivatives trading, DCM origination, and institutional structuring interviews.

1. Explain how an Interest Rate Swap works and why the notional is never exchanged. +

Answer: An interest rate swap (IRS) is an agreement between two parties to exchange periodic interest payments on an agreed notional principal ($N$) over a specified tenor:

  • The Fixed Payer pays a predetermined fixed coupon and receives a floating reference rate (e.g. SOFR).
  • The Fixed Receiver pays the floating reference rate and receives the fixed coupon.

Why Notional is Never Exchanged: Both parties are denominating interest payments in the same currency. Exchanging $100M at inception and returning the exact same $100M at maturity would consume balance sheet capacity without altering interest rate risk. Instead, on each payment date, the cash flows are netted: the party owing more simply wires the net differential: $$\text{Net Cash Flow} = N \times (L - R_{\text{fixed}}) \times \alpha$$

2. How do you calculate the Par Swap Rate from discount factors? +

Answer: At inception, the swap has zero Net Present Value ($NPV = 0$), meaning the present value of the fixed leg must equal the present value of the floating leg:

PV(\text{Fixed Leg}) = PV(\text{Floating Leg})

Since a floating-rate note paying benchmark coupons is worth par ($1.00$) at inception and redeems principal at date $T$, the present value of its coupons alone is $1 - DF_T$.

R_{\text{par}} \times \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i DF_i = 1 - DF_n \implies \mathbf{R_{\text{par}} = \frac{1 - DF_n}{\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i DF_i}}

Trader Tip: The denominator $\sum \alpha_i DF_i$ is known as the Swap Annuity ($A_n$) or the PV01 per dollar notional.
3. What is a Swap Spread, and why have 10-year and 30-year US swap spreads traded negative? +

Answer: A Swap Spread is defined as:

\text{Swap Spread} = \text{Par Swap Rate} - \text{Treasury Yield of Same Maturity}

Historically, swap spreads were always positive because swaps involved interbank credit risk. However, 10Y and 30Y swap spreads turned negative post-2015 due to three structural factors:

  1. Massive Treasury Issuance: Huge US federal borrowing flooded the market with long-end Treasuries, pushing Treasury yields above swap rates.
  2. Bank Balance Sheet Regulations (SLR / Basel III): Holding physical Treasury bonds consumes scarce bank balance sheet leverage. In contrast, cleared swaps do not require financing cash on balance sheet, making swaps cheaper than Treasuries.
  3. Pension & LDI Hedging: Defined-benefit pension funds heavily receive fixed on 30Y swaps to match long liability duration, depressing long swap rates.
4. What is OIS discounting, and why did global derivatives desks abandon Libor discounting? +

Answer: An Overnight Index Swap (OIS) exchanges fixed rate payments for the compounded daily risk-free overnight rate (SOFR in USD, €STR in EUR).

Why OIS Discounting was Adopted:

  • Under standard ISDA Credit Support Annexes (CSAs), counterparties must post cash collateral against out-of-the-money swap positions daily.
  • The interest paid on this posted cash collateral is the overnight rate (SOFR/€STR).
  • Under no-arbitrage finance, cash flows must be discounted at the return earned on the collateral that backs them. Therefore, the OIS curve is the true risk-free discount curve for all collateralized derivatives.
5. A bank holds $500M of fixed-rate mortgages funded by floating-rate deposits. How does it hedge using a swap? +

Answer: The bank faces asset-liability mismatch:

  • Assets: Receive fixed mortgage interest (e.g. 5.50%).
  • Liabilities: Pay floating deposit interest (e.g. SOFR). If interest rates spike, deposit costs jump while mortgage revenues stay flat, destroying bank net interest margin (NIM).

The Hedge: The bank enters a Payer Swap (Pay Fixed, Receive Floating) for $500M notional:

  • The floating cash received from the swap offsets the rising deposit payments.
  • The fixed payment paid on the swap is funded by the mortgage income.
  • The bank successfully locks in a guaranteed net interest margin spread regardless of where rates move.
6. What is the DV01 of a 10-year $100M receiver swap, and what is its P&L if 10Y swap rates fall by 20 bps? +

Answer:

A 10-year swap has a typical swap annuity $A_{10} \approx 7.90$ (sum of semi-annual discount factors).

\text{DV01} = \text{Notional} \times 0.0001 \times A_{10} = \$100,000,000 \times 0.0001 \times 7.90 = \mathbf{\$79,000\text{ per bp}}

A Receiver Swap benefits when market rates fall (it locked in higher fixed receipts).

\text{P\&L} = +\text{DV01} \times (-\Delta y_{\text{bps}}) = \$79,000 \times (+20\text{ bps}) = \mathbf{+\$1,580,000}

The desk makes an immediate mark-to-market gain of $1.58 million.

7. What is the duration of an Interest Rate Swap, and why does a payer swap have negative duration? +

Answer: A swap can be decomposed into two synthetic bonds:

\text{Payer Swap} = \text{Long Floating Rate Note} + \text{Short Fixed Rate Bond}

The duration of a floating-rate note between reset dates is approximately the time to the next reset (e.g. 0.25 years for quarterly SOFR). The duration of a 10-year fixed bond is ~7.8 years.

D_{\text{payer}} \approx D_{\text{floating}} - D_{\text{fixed}} \approx 0.25 - 7.8 = \mathbf{-7.55\text{ Years}}

Because the short fixed leg dominates, a payer swap has large negative duration (its value rises when yields rise, exactly like a short bond position).

8. What is a Cross-Currency Basis Swap and what causes a non-zero basis? +

Answer: A Cross-Currency Basis Swap involves exchanging floating interest rate payments and principal amounts in two different currencies (e.g. 3M USD SOFR vs 3M EUR €STR).

Under textbook Covered Interest Parity (CIP), the basis spread should be zero. In practice:

  • Non-Zero Basis: The EUR/USD and JPY/USD basis spreads have historically traded substantially negative (e.g. -20 to -60 bps).
  • Cause: Massive structural global demand for US dollar funding from foreign banks and institutional investors, combined with bank balance sheet constraints that prevent arbitrageurs from closing the gap. Foreign borrowers must pay a premium to borrow USD synthetically.
9. How does a rates trading desk structure a 2s10s curve steepener trade using swaps? +

Answer: In a 2s10s curve steepener, the trader profits if the 10Y swap rate rises relative to the 2Y swap rate:

  1. Legs: Receive Fixed on 2Y swap (benefits from short rates falling) and Pay Fixed on 10Y swap (benefits from long rates rising).
  2. DV01-Neutral Weighting: To avoid losing money if the whole curve moves in parallel, the trade must have zero net DV01: $$\text{Notional}_{2\text{Y}} \times \text{DV01}_{2\text{Y}} = \text{Notional}_{10\text{Y}} \times \text{DV01}_{10\text{Y}}$$
  3. Ratio: If a 2Y swap has a DV01 of $1.90 per $10k and a 10Y swap has a DV01 of $7.90 per $10k, the notional ratio is: $$\frac{\text{Notional}_{2\text{Y}}}{\text{Notional}_{10\text{Y}}} = \frac{7.90}{1.90} \approx 4.16$$ The desk receives fixed on $416M of 2Y swaps for every $100M of 10Y swaps paid.
10. What is CSA discounting, and how does the choice of collateral currency impact swap valuation? +

Answer: Under a Credit Support Annex (CSA), the currency of posted cash collateral dictates the discount curve used for valuation:

  • If collateral is posted in USD cash, the discount rate is USD SOFR.
  • If collateral is posted in EUR cash, the discount rate is EUR €STR.
  • Collateral Option (Cheapest-to-Deliver Collateral): Many legacy multi-currency CSAs gave the posting counterparty the right to choose which currency to post (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY). Desks had to model this as an embedded currency option, discounting at the "cheapest-to-deliver" collateral rate, which significantly impacted the mark-to-market valuation of long-dated swap books!
